CRU — Canonical Representation Unit

For decades, digital representations have depended on the systems around them—databases, platforms, repositories, networks, and authorities.

CRU explores a different possibility:

What if the representation itself could become the trusted primitive?

A CRU is designed to create a portable, verifiable digital representation that can remain meaningful across systems and environments—without being defined solely by where it is stored, who hosts it, or which system recognizes it.

Portable. Verifiable. Independent by design.

A new primitive for a distributed world.

Dench Labs is exploring what becomes possible when digital representations can stand on their own.

The architecture of a CRU.

The CRU architecture rests on five foundational pillars. Together, they establish the CRU as a foundational primitive for decentralized governance.

01

One authoritative representation of an entity.

02

03 / ZTA VS CRU

Zero Trust secures access. CRU governs capability.

CRU does not reject Zero Trust. Zero Trust establishes powerful security principles; CRU provides a computational architectural primitive in which those principles can become intrinsic to the units performing work.

ZERO TRUST ARCHITECTURE
Identity → Policy → Continuous Verification → Access / Resource

Identity, device posture, context, policy, monitoring, least privilege, and micro-segmentation determine whether access should be granted and maintained.

CRU ARCHITECTURE
Canonical Representation → Integrity → Persistence → Capability Composition → Interoperability

Within the CRU, identity, authorization, execution, state, and governance become coupled to the representation and its capabilities.

Continuous monitoring

ZTA continuously evaluates trust and access conditions. CRU extends this into the lifecycle of the capability itself.

Least privilege

ZTA asks what minimum access is required. CRU asks what minimum capability is required to perform the specific operation.

Micro-segmentation

ZTA can segment networks, workloads, devices, and applications. CRU makes fine-grained segmentation intrinsic to the CRU layer.

Capabilities

A CRU capability can be narrowly scoped, time-limited, condition-dependent, revocable, auditable, governed, and bound to execution.

05 / CAPABILITY IN CONTEXT

Capability in Context

Capability is not a static property of identity. In a CRU architecture, the capability that may be exercised is evaluated against the current identity, device state, mission, requested action, and governing conditions.

Elevated Access

When Alice's mission changes, she can receive a narrowly scoped, temporary capability rather than a permanent elevated role. A bazooka capability can be authorized for a particular mission, under specified conditions, for a defined duration, and only while the relevant device and environmental state remains compliant.

Normal:
  Toy Gun       ACTIVE
  Real Gun      ACTIVE
  Bazooka       DENIED

Elevated:
  Toy Gun       ACTIVE
  Real Gun      ACTIVE
  Bazooka       CONDITIONALLY ACTIVE
    Mission = X
    Authorization = Y
    Duration = Limited
    Device State = Compliant

When the governing condition ends, the capability expires or is revoked. Alice's identity did not permanently change; her governed capability set changed.

CIDD — Decision Over Capability in Context

A CIDD decision evaluates the current context rather than treating authorization as a permanent identity attribute. The decision can incorporate identity, device, mission, current state, requested capability, and governance policy.

Identity + Device + Mission + State
        + Requested Capability + Governance
                    ↓
                   CIDD
                    ↓
           Capability Decision

Eve Impersonates Alice

Suppose Eve obtains Alice's credentials and presents herself as Alice. An identity claim alone should not automatically confer Alice's governed capabilities. The CRU model distinguishes an identity assertion from the capability composition authorized for the governed computational unit.

Compromised Device State

If Alice's authenticated device or surrounding context becomes compromised, the system can reevaluate her capabilities without redefining her identity. Different capabilities can move to different states.

Elevated Access While Eve Impersonates Alice

Consider the harder case: Charlie authorizes Alice for temporary bazooka access, and Eve then attempts to impersonate Alice. Eve may possess Alice's username, password, or session artifacts, but those artifacts are not equivalent to Alice's governed capability composition.

Alice
  └── Alice-CRU
       └── Temporary Bazooka Capability
            └── Mission X
                 └── Limited Duration

Eve
  └── Eve-CRU
       └── No Bazooka Capability

Fine-Grained Segmentation

Traditional segmentation can isolate training, operational, and strategic networks. CRU-level segmentation can go further by treating individual capabilities as governed boundaries. Each capability can have its own identity, authorization, state, execution, and governance conditions.

Alice-CRU
  ├── Toy Gun Capability
  ├── Real Gun Capability
  └── Bazooka Capability
Core takeaway: Identity establishes who you claim to be. Capability establishes what you are authorized to do. Governance establishes when and under what conditions you may do it. CRU binds these concepts to the computational unit that actually performs the action.
