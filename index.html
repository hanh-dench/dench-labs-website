CANONICAL REPRESENTATION UNIT

Governed Computational Objects

Dench Labs researches foundational infrastructure for computational objects that can carry verifiable identity, state, integrity, and governance independent of where they are stored or executed.

THE QUESTION

What if a digital object could carry its own truth?

Most computational objects depend on external systems to determine what they are, whether they are authentic, what state they are in, and what they are permitted to do.

Move the object away from those systems and much of that meaning can disappear.

CRU asks a different question: What if the object could carry enough verifiable information and governance with it to remain meaningful wherever it goes?

That is the foundation of the Canonical Representation Unit.

WHAT IS A CRU?

The governed object persists.

A CRU is a canonical representation of a computational object whose identity, integrity, state, provenance, and governing conditions can be independently evaluated.

It is not defined by a particular database, blockchain, cloud provider, application, or transport.

The infrastructure may change.
The governed object persists.

The architecture of a CRU.

Canonical Representation

A deterministic representation of the object and its governed state.

Cryptographic Integrity

CRU + ZERO TRUST

Complementary architectures.

CRU is not a replacement for Zero Trust. Zero Trust provides an architectural approach for continuously evaluating access based on identity, policy, context, and risk.

CRU introduces a governed computational object that can participate in that architecture while carrying verifiable identity, state, provenance, and governance across systems.

Zero Trust asks: Should this request be allowed under current conditions?

CRU adds: What governed object is making the request, what is its current state, and what governed result applies?

BOUNDARY

What CRU does — and does not claim.

CRU does not eliminate trust. It does not replace cryptography, PKI, identity infrastructure, Zero Trust, databases, blockchains, operating systems, or existing enforcement mechanisms.

Instead, CRU explores a different computational boundary: the object itself can become a persistent unit at which identity, state, verification, capability, and governance are expressed.

Existing infrastructure can then evaluate and act upon that governed object.
