ABOUT DENCH LABS

Foundational systems research.

Dench Labs researches foundational infrastructure for persistent, verifiable, and governed computational objects. The objective is not to replace useful infrastructure, but to explore what becomes possible when the governed object can persist independently of it.

ABOUT

Research First

Computing infrastructure changes continuously. Applications, cloud providers, networks, databases, security architectures, and organizations change.

A foundational computational object should not necessarily lose its identity or governance simply because the infrastructure around it changes.

Research first. Infrastructure that endures.
PRINCIPLE

Technology Neutrality

A CRU might operate within a company, university, government, military organization, nonprofit, healthcare institution, distributed community, or another governed environment. Those organizations may have very different policies. CRU does not require them to become the same.

Technology provides mechanism. The governing domain provides policy.

PRINCIPLE

Sovereignty of the Person

A person may interact with many institutions during a lifetime. Those relationships should not inherently make the person’s computational identity the property of any single institution, platform, repository, or application.

CRU research distinguishes between continuity of the person and authority exercised by institutions within their legitimate domains.

Here, sovereignty of the person refers to continuity and autonomy of personal identity across computational systems. It is not a claim of political sovereignty.

PRINCIPLE

Where consent is applicable, it should be meaningful rather than merely implied by technical participation. Where an organization possesses legitimate responsibility within a domain, its authority should still be identifiable and bounded.

PRINCIPLE

Organizational Freedom Within Boundaries

Companies can establish company policy. Governments can exercise lawful governmental authority. Universities can establish academic governance. Communities can establish participation rules. Operational organizations can establish mission authority.

CRU provides a framework in which authority can be represented, evaluated, and bounded without prescribing one universal policy model.

GOVERNANCE PRINCIPLES

Five principles.

01

Authority Must Be Explicit

Authority should be identifiable rather than silently inferred from technical control.

02

Authority Must Be Bounded

Possessing authority in one context should not automatically create authority elsewhere.

03

Governance Must Be Attributable

It should be possible to understand what governance produced a result and under whose authority.

04

Participation and Consent Should Be Respected Where Applicable

Governed systems should distinguish legitimate institutional authority from situations in which participation or consent is required.

05

Technology Enables Governance — It Does Not Legitimize Authority

Cryptography can prove. Software can evaluate. Systems can enforce. None alone determines whether the underlying authority is legitimate.

RESPONSIBLE DEVELOPMENT

Power requires boundaries.

Dench Labs considers risks including concentration of authority, surveillance, unintended federation, privilege escalation, loss of meaningful consent, cross-domain compromise, governance capture, and authority extending beyond its intended context.

Security therefore includes more than protecting cryptographic keys or network connections. It also means protecting the boundaries within which authority is allowed to operate.
